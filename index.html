Executive Narrative
What we are building, why it matters, and what leadership needs to align on

Celtra does not need a collection of AI experiments. We need a governed signal-to-action operating model for GTM.

Revenue Operations should help turn scattered GTM activity into coordinated execution: identify meaningful customer and market signals, decide which role owns the next action, provide trusted context, route work through the systems teams already use, and measure whether the action improved conversion, adoption, expansion, retention, or forecast quality.

Strategic bet

Snowflake should become Celtra's governed GTM data record and trust layer. Account, product, customer, support, commercial, engagement, and outcome signals should be modeled there, then exposed through controlled workflows in Salesforce, Slack, Gmail, HubSpot, Claude, and dashboards.

Operating posture

Salesforce remains the revenue operating surface. RevOps should not create a second source of truth. We should design workflows that use Salesforce for account ownership, tasks, opportunity/customer context, and outcome logging while Snowflake becomes the trusted data foundation underneath.

Question 01

What changed?

Every workflow starts with a named signal: buyer engagement, trial activity, usage change, stakeholder movement, support risk, commercial blocker, or renewal window.

Question 02

Who owns action?

Marketing, AE, SC, CSM, Legal, Deal Desk, Support, or leadership owns the next step based on the signal and account context.

Question 03

Did it work?

Recommendations must produce measurable outcomes: meetings, stage progression, trial conversion, expansion pipeline, risk reduction, or forecast hygiene.

Operating Plan
How RevOps pilots controlled workflows while the data foundation matures

The path is controlled pilots now, governed infrastructure next.

RevOps can create near-term value by piloting human-reviewed workflows in Salesforce, Slack, Gmail, HubSpot, and Claude using trusted existing data. The scalable version requires DI and functional leadership alignment on Snowflake-modeled signals, identity resolution, permissions, recommendation logging, and adoption accountability.

Phase 01 · Decide

Define the operating rules

Align RevOps, DI, Sales, Marketing, CS, Product, Legal, and Finance on source-of-truth rules, signal taxonomy, account identity, AI access, human approval boundaries, and where recommendations and outcomes should be logged.

Phase 02 · Pilot

Run controlled front-end workflows

Use Salesforce tasks and fields, Slack alerts, Gmail drafts, HubSpot workflows, Gong summaries, Zendesk context, and Claude-generated briefs. Keep humans in control and measure recommendation acceptance, completion, and outcomes.

Phase 03 · Scale

Promote reliable signals into infrastructure

Move validated signals into Snowflake-modeled definitions, retire manual workarounds, document field and prompt rules, and decide which tools deserve investment based on unique signal, context, execution, or measurement value.

RevOps ownership

RevOps owns the GTM workflow layer: use-case intake, pilot prioritization, Salesforce workflow design, prompt/business-rule documentation, user adoption, action measurement, and tool rationalization. RevOps can move before the full architecture is complete, but should avoid brittle automations that duplicate the future data layer.

Governance boundary

Agents can summarize, recommend, draft, route, score, and prioritize. They cannot autonomously negotiate pricing, send unrestricted outbound communication, modify contracts, or update critical customer/commercial data. Customer-facing sends, contract changes, pricing actions, and critical record updates require human approval.

Stack Architecture
Tools are organized by job: signal source, commercial truth, intelligence, execution, and measurement across Marketing, AE, SC, and CS workflows

Use the Agentic Enterprise architecture as the organizing model.

The stack should be explained as four connected systems: the system of engagement where users interact, the system of agency where agents reason and recommend, the system of work where business processes execute, and the system of context where governed data, enrichment, product usage, and conversation history live.

Success Metrics
Measure whether signal-based workflows create better timing, better context, and better revenue outcomes

Success should be measured by action quality and revenue impact, not by the volume of AI recommendations.

The goal is not to create more alerts. The goal is to create fewer, better-timed actions that sellers, SCs, and CSMs trust enough to act on, and that answer the executive test: did this improve conversion, adoption, expansion, retention, or forecast quality?

Timing metrics

Time from signal to action, speed to follow-up, stage aging reduction, renewal-risk lead time, and whether users act before the window of value closes.

Context metrics

Signal precision, false-positive rate, duplicate alert rate, data completeness, source traceability, and whether recommendations point to the correct owner with enough context to act.

Action metrics

Recommendation acceptance, action completion, meetings booked, opportunity creation, stage progression, expansion pipeline, renewal risk reduction, forecast hygiene, and close-plan readiness.

Evaluate · Observe · Optimize
Evaluate

Before launch, test whether each workflow has complete data, the right owner, an explainable recommendation, a clear action, and an acceptable false-positive rate.

Observe

After launch, monitor recommendation acceptance, ignored alerts, prompt quality, data failures, workflow latency, and whether users take the recommended action.

Continuously optimize

Review real conversations and action outcomes. Tune prompts, signal thresholds, routing rules, source data, and success metrics before expanding automation.
