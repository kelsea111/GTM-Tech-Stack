Executive Thesis
Move from isolated activity to coordinated, signal-driven revenue execution

We are not building a bigger sales tech stack. We are building a cross-functional signal-to-action revenue system.

The competitive advantage is not having AI. It is knowing how to operationalize AI into repeatable, measurable workflows. Salesforce remains the operating system for AE, Solutions Consulting, and Customer Success work. Snowflake unifies product, customer, and commercial data. Claude/agentic workflows interpret signals, generate role-specific recommendations, and route actions through Salesforce tasks, Gmail, Slack, HubSpot, Gong, Zendesk, Pendo, and SpotDraft only when there is a clear reason to act.

Team question

Who needs to act?

Prioritized AE, SC, and CSM actions based on buyer engagement, product usage, support risk, trial health, commercial readiness, and renewal timing.

Timing question

Why now?

Every action is tied to a named trigger: campaign engagement, demo request, trial usage, technical blocker, support escalation, renewal risk, expansion signal, or stage stall.

Execution question

What should we do?

Agent recommends the play, drafts the message or brief, routes to the right owner, coordinates the pod, and logs the outcome for learning.

Updated understanding

The GTM stack should not optimize one role in isolation. AE, Solutions Consulting, and Customer Success each own different moments in the customer journey, but they should work from the same account context, signal definitions, action queue, and outcome measurement. The goal is coordinated revenue execution from demand creation through demo, trial, close, onboarding, expansion, and renewal.

Operating principle

Every workflow must answer five questions: What signal changed? Which role owns the next action? What context do they need? Which channel should execute it? Did it improve conversion, adoption, expansion, retention, or forecast quality? If a tool does not materially improve one of those answers, it is not strategic.

Agentic Operating Model
Move from AI pilots to a governed revenue capability

Agentic selling is not a one-time workflow build. It is an operating capability.

The company should start with specific business outcomes, build around real user workflows, test recommendations continuously, and assign ownership for prompts, data definitions, releases, security, and measurement. The goal is not to launch more agents; the goal is to create a reliable system that improves revenue execution.

01 · Assist

Summarize and draft

Meeting briefs, account summaries, call recaps, stakeholder notes, and first-draft emails. Primary KPI: hours saved and prep completion.

02 · Act

Execute bounded tasks

Create Salesforce tasks, draft Gmail follow-ups, update fields, route Slack alerts, and suggest Outreach sequence enrollment. Primary KPI: tasks completed.

03 · Assure

Operate with guardrails

Human-reviewed recommendations, approval gates, source-of-truth rules, false-positive review, and prompt/data testing. Primary KPI: recommendation accuracy and accepted actions.

04 · Orchestrate

Coordinate across teams

AE, SC, CSM, Legal, Deal Desk, and Support workflows connected through shared account context and owner-specific actions. Primary KPI: resolution time and cross-functional handoff completion.

05 · Transform

Optimize business outcomes

Agents influence pipeline creation, trial conversion, expansion, retention, forecast hygiene, and renewal readiness. Primary KPI: revenue impact, churn reduction, and customer experience improvement.

Build

Start with low-risk, high-value workflows where the user remains in control: meeting prep, post-meeting follow-up, stalled opportunity recovery, demo readiness, and renewal readiness.

Operate

Review real recommendations and conversations weekly. Track ignored alerts, false positives, bad data, prompt issues, and actions that failed to produce outcomes.

Scale

Only promote a workflow into automation when the signal is reliable, the owner is clear, the action is accepted, and the business impact is measurable.

Stack Architecture
Tools are organized by job: signal source, commercial truth, intelligence, execution, and measurement across AE, SC, and CS workflows

Use the Agentic Enterprise architecture as the organizing model.

The stack should be explained as four connected systems: the system of engagement where users interact, the system of agency where agents reason and recommend, the system of work where business processes execute, and the system of context where governed data, enrichment, product usage, and conversation history live.

System of engagement

Where users experience the agentic workflow: Salesforce UI, Slack, Gmail, dashboards, and manager views.

System of agency

Where AI reasons and recommends: Claude / Agentforce-style agents, prompts, play selection, routing logic, and human approval steps.

System of work

Where business processes execute: Salesforce, Outreach, HubSpot, Zendesk, SpotDraft, Gmail, Slack, and Salesforce Flow.

System of context

Where trusted context comes from: Snowflake, Pendo, Gong, MediaRadar, Adomik, Clay, LeadIQ, Sales Nav, Salesforce history, and customer data.

From Pilot to Operating Capability
Build the signal operating model while establishing governance, testing, ownership, and data readiness

The roadmap should turn AI experimentation into a durable operating capability.

Signal-based selling will only scale if the company defines a durable data layer, governance model, integration pattern, testing discipline, release process, and AI operating architecture. RevOps does not need to wait for every foundational decision to be complete, but it should avoid building brittle automations that duplicate the future data layer or create a second source of truth outside Salesforce and Snowflake.

Phase 01 · Days 1-30

Define the architecture decisions

Align RevOps, DI, Sales, CS, Product, and Finance on the foundational choices that determine whether signal-based AI can scale: canonical account model, data ownership, identity resolution, source-of-truth rules, AI access patterns, governance, and where recommendations should be surfaced.

Phase 02 · Days 31-60

Build RevOps-controlled front-end workflows

Use Salesforce-native fields, tasks, views, dashboards, flows, Slack alerts, and human-reviewed Claude briefs to pilot the operating model without waiting for full data infrastructure. Focus on workflows that use existing reliable data: HubSpot engagement, Salesforce opportunity hygiene, Gong summaries, Zendesk escalations, and manually validated usage signals.

Phase 03 · Days 61-90

Transition pilots into governed infrastructure

Compare pilot outcomes against the emerging data architecture. Promote validated triggers into Snowflake-modeled signals, retire manual workarounds, document field definitions, and decide whether external signal tooling like Clay, Sales Navigator, or Outreach is still needed based on measurable workflow value.

Foundational AI architecture decisions

Before scaling signal-based AI, the company should decide:

  • Canonical account and contact model: which object IDs, hierarchies, domains, parent/child relationships, and buyer roles define the customer graph.
  • Source-of-truth rules: which system owns account data, product usage, contract status, support risk, billing status, engagement, and outcome history.
  • Identity resolution: how contacts, users, domains, product users, buying committee members, and customer stakeholders are matched across systems.
  • Signal taxonomy: what counts as a usage signal, engagement signal, stakeholder signal, deal risk, support risk, expansion signal, or renewal risk.
  • Data freshness expectations: which signals need real-time updates, daily refresh, weekly refresh, or manual validation.
  • AI access pattern: whether agents query Snowflake directly, call governed APIs, read from Salesforce, or operate through approved semantic models.
  • Governance and permissions: what customer, product, contract, support, and email data AI can access; how PII and confidential data are protected.
  • Recommendation logging: where AI recommendations, seller acceptance, dismissals, edits, actions, and outcomes are stored for learning.
  • Human-in-the-loop rules: which actions AI may draft, recommend, route, update, or execute; and which actions require seller, CSM, manager, Legal, or Finance approval.
What RevOps can do without owning the full architecture

RevOps can still create meaningful front-end value while DI builds the data layer:

  • Create Salesforce fields for Signal Type, Signal Source, Recommended Play, Action Owner, Action Status, Dismissal Reason, and Outcome.
  • Build prioritized Salesforce list views for AEs, SCs, and CSMs using trusted existing fields.
  • Use Salesforce Flow for simple routing before adding separate orchestration tooling.
  • Generate Claude-assisted briefs from Salesforce, Gong summaries, HubSpot activity, Zendesk case summaries, and manually reviewed Snowflake/Pendo exports.
  • Create Slack alerts that link back to Salesforce records instead of letting recommendations live only in Slack.
  • Run controlled pilots with human review before automating any customer-facing action.

Success Metrics
Measure whether signal-based workflows create better timing, better context, and better revenue outcomes

Success should be measured by action quality and revenue impact, not by the volume of AI recommendations.

The goal is not to create more alerts. The goal is to create fewer, better-timed actions that sellers, SCs, and CSMs trust enough to act on — and that improve conversion, adoption, expansion, retention, and forecast quality.

Evaluate

Before launch, test whether each workflow has complete data, the right owner, an explainable recommendation, a clear action, and an acceptable false-positive rate.

Observe

After launch, monitor recommendation acceptance, ignored alerts, prompt quality, data failures, workflow latency, and whether users take the recommended action.

Continuously optimize

Review real conversations and action outcomes. Tune prompts, signal thresholds, routing rules, source data, and success metrics before expanding automation.
